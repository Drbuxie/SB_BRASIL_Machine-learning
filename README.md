**Background:** Social and household conditions are associated with oral health, but their ability to classify multiple oral health outcomes in population-based adult samples is not well established. We compared six machine-learning algorithms and examined predictor contributions for three outcomes among Brazilian adults.
**Methods:** We analyzed cross-sectional data from SB Brazil 2023 survey. Adults aged 18 years or older who were neither edentulous nor had missing outcome or predictor data were included. The final analytic sample included 11,453 participants. Outcomes were untreated dental caries, periodontal disease, and poor oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL), defined using k-means clustering of the OIDP score. Logistic regression, LASSO, gradient boosting machine (GBM), linear XGBoost, random forest, and a neural network were evaluated on held-out test sets. Models were selected by considering the Brier score and other performance metrics jointly. SHAP analysis summarized predictor contributions in the selected models.
**Results:** Untreated caries affected 51.4% of participants (n=5,889), periodontal disease 44.0% (n=5,045), and poor OHRQoL 21.0% (n=2,404). Test-set AUROC ranged from 0.739 to 0.743 for caries, 0.604 to 0.630 for the periodontal outcome, and 0.710 to 0.760 for OHRQoL. LASSO was selected for caries, GBM for the periodontal outcome, and XGBoost for OHRQoL; their Brier scores were 0.2055, 0.2338, and 0.1409, respectively. Self-perceived oral health had the largest grouped SHAP contribution for all three outcomes. Other high-ranking predictors varied by outcome and included reason for dental consultation, minimum-wage category, periodontal status, caries status, dental-service use, and education.
**Conclusions:** Machine learning models captured differential patterns of social, demographic, behavioral, and healthcare-related predictors across multiple oral health outcomes. Self-perceived oral health consistently contributed most strongly to model predictions, highlighting its potential as an important population-level indicator of oral health inequalities.

**Keywords:** Social Determinants of Health; Dental Caries; Periodontal Diseases; Quality of Life; Machine Learning
